# Grounded reference library

Approved examples for Half Byte sites, with Jake's notes from the 24 Nov 2026 grounded-agency review.
These are the bar. Award-site findings still explain craft (scale contrast, photography, one signature moment),
but the **target band** is polished local-business agency work a homeowner is comfortable on — not Awwwards
experimental. Do not copy text, photos, or components from any reference.

Full write-up: the Page "What makes modern websites stand out."

## How to use this library

- **Top tier** is the bar to beat. When in doubt, aim here (especially Eppright, Yardie, Nature's Call, Buchanan, Maple Crest).
- **Safe but sellable** is the floor. A first run must clear this. It can be "totally sellable" and still must pass the template smell test.
- **Opt-in directions** are used only when Jake or the plan names them. They are not the default for a typical local-service client.
- **Rejected** examples are anti-references. If a layout starts to look like one of them, it fails review.

## Opt-in directions (not the default)

Use only when Jake asks, or when the plan explicitly names the direction for a client it fits.

### Premium / editorial — Cormack (1)

- Site: https://www.cormackdevelopments.com.au/
- Jake: "incredibly cool, want this as a skill option but be selective about which clients get it."
- Borrow: olive or tonal color bands; a small, calm headline over a dark, photographic hero; a luxurious, quiet pace.
- Do not borrow: a horizontal-scroll project rail, or a custom "View" cursor.
- Fits: a high-end custom builder or designer who already has cinematic photos and wants a quieter, more exclusive feel. Not the default for a typical local trade or a first-run family builder.

### Playful / retro — Thelen (13)

- Site: https://callthelen.com/
- Jake: "LOVE the playful retro vibe."
- Borrow: a retro badge brand, a promo bar only when the offer is real, schedule + phone CTAs, a star-rating strip with towns served.
- Fits: a personality-forward trade (HVAC, plumbing, a shop with a real mascot or vintage identity). Not the default for a custom-home or luxury-modern builder.

## Rejected as template — anti-references

If a build starts to look like one of these, it fails the template smell test, even if it is "clean."

### 2. Legacy Builders — https://www.legacybldrs.com/

Jake rejected it as template. A centered, contained hero photo (a polite rectangle in the page, not a full-bleed job) plus a "Portfolio by Rooms" identical tile grid. Clear and plain. It reads as a template because every section is the same contained photo+caption tile, the hero is a boxed image, and nothing is specific to one house or one owner.

### 8. Teton Heritage Builders — https://www.tetonheritagebuilders.com/

Jake rejected it as template. A years-in-business emblem and award badges stacked on the hero, plus an association-logo strip. It feels dated. It reads as a template because badges and logos do the work that photography and a real story should do, and the "mountain luxury" chrome (badge soup, heavy prestige lockups) could be swapped onto any resort builder.

### 9. Hammond Design Build — https://www.hammonddesignbuild.com/

Jake rejected it as template. A phone pill and a customer-portal link sitting on an otherwise standard remodeler layout. It reads as a template because the only designed objects are chrome, while the page structure (generic hero, icon service grid, interchangeable why-us) could be swapped to any kitchen-and-bath firm without changing the layout's meaning.

## Template smell test

Run this in review, in addition to the anti-generic tells in design-foundation A20. Fail the build if two or more are true and the plan did not justify them by name.

**From the rejected set:**

- A centered, boxed hero photo inset in the container instead of a full-bleed real job photo (Legacy).
- An identical tile grid of rooms, services, or projects with the same ratio, same chrome, same caption pattern (Legacy).
- Award, year, or association badges stacked on the hero as a substitute for a featured house or an owner story (Teton).
- A logo-strip of associations or suppliers doing the work of proof, with no real projects beside them (Teton).
- Dated "prestige" chrome: heavy badge emblems, stock-feeling luxury lockups, a theme that could be any mountain or coastal builder (Teton).
- The only designed header objects are a phone pill or a portal link, on top of a standard remodeler skeleton (Hammond).
- Icon service grids and generic why-us blocks that could be copied to another firm unchanged (Hammond, and Buchanan's why-choose grid, which we skip even though we like the rest of Buchanan).

**Still in force from A20:** eyebrow on every heading; cream + serif + terracotta as a default; fade-up on every section; count-up stat rows; wandering left edges; wildly off-center type.

## What the strongest approved sites share (kit defaults)

- One centered container (about 1240–1280px) and the same side padding in every mid-page section. Text never floats off the grid. Only the hero, one dark band, and at most one photo band run full-bleed.
- The hero is a full-bleed real job photo, with the headline left- or center-aligned *inside the container*, one or two CTAs, and the phone in the header.
- Display type at 1440 is about **56–88px** for most sites (tight leading, slight negative tracking). Body 16–18px. A luxury-modern builder in the Eppright vein may go up to **120px** when the plan names that knob. Do not default to 150px display lines.
- A trust strip sits right under the hero: Google rating and review count, years, licence or warranty, associations — only confirmed facts.
- Projects are photos inside the container: one big feature photo with a small info card (Eppright), or one big plus two small with name and town captions (Nature's Call), or a calm 2×2 / 3-column photo grid (Buchanan, Maple Crest). Not a typographic hover takeover, and not a Legacy-style identical tile grid.
- The owners appear on a real job site with a short story in their own voice, when we have the photos and the words.
- The page runs through a 3–6 step process, reviews, a service area with town names, an FAQ, and a dark closing CTA band with a form or the phone.
- Asymmetry is restrained and grid-snapped: 50/50 or 60/40 splits, a heading-left/intro-right header row, one big plus two small.
- Motion is quiet. Soft reveals on a few blocks at most, ~180ms hovers, one signature moment, reduced motion honored.
- Header: a short nav, the phone, and a CTA. Sticky call bar on phones.
