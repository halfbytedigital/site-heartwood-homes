# Copy voice (public site)

Write like the owners talking to a homeowner: short, concrete, and plain. For a Southern trades
company, Southern-plain — Daniel and Laura, not a marketing desk. Facts come from intake and the
source crawl. Missing facts are omitted or reworded naturally. Never explain a gap to the visitor.

## Hard rules

- No internal kit or sourcing language on the public site. The visitor should never hear how we
  gathered facts, what a previous site "already lists," or what we are still waiting on.
- Do not narrate a missing fact ("we don't publish a price," "a cost range needs numbers from
  Heartwood before it can be shown," "no prices are listed here," "we don't have a response time").
  If there is no price or no timing promise, just don't mention the absence. Say what does happen:
  "Plans, then an estimate, then we build." "A few questions first. Then we'll call or write you back."
- Do not label the writing as honest ("honest cost guidance," "an honest look"). Be plain instead.
- No generic agency filler, stacked triads ("quality, integrity, and excellence"), or em-dash cadence
  in body copy. Prefer a period or a comma.
- Do not talk about the website ("every house is a page") when you should be talking about the work.

## Banned or flagged phrases

`review.mjs` flags these in visible copy (and close variants). Rewrite or delete before review.

**Internal / meta (how we know, what's missing, kit-speak):**
already lists, what we list, what [Name] already lists, sourced, confirmed, placeholder, not
published, we don't have, we do not have, we don't publish, do not publish, does not list a price,
no prices are published, nothing on this site is a price, nothing here is a published price, no
prices are listed here, listed together on the current site, the current site, a cost range needs
numbers, we don't publish a response time, we don't ask for a budget until

**Self-labels and filler:**
honest cost, honest guidance, an honest look, crafted with care, quality craftsmanship, attention
to detail, seamless, elevate / elevated / elevating, nestled, bespoke, unparalleled, timeless
elegance, dream home, "whether you're … or …"

**Cadence tells:** em dashes (—) in body copy; triad headlines.

If a phrase is the client's own sourced wording (for example a values sentence on their current
site), keep the meaning, still cut kit-speak, and prefer how they would say it out loud.
